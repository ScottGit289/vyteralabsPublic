After prostate surgery you will be told to do pelvic floor exercises three times a day, for at least three months. Almost nobody does. Anchor is a quiet timer, a reminder that actually arrives, and a record you can hand to your physiotherapist.

Built for where you are

Before surgery. Catheter still in. Catheter just out. Months down the line. Anchor asks, and sets a starting programme to match.

If your catheter is still in, it holds the programme — because UK guidance is to start active exercises once it has been removed. Your team decides when, not an app.

A set, done discreetly

A calm ring counts you through long squeezes and quick squeezes. Vibration-only cues as standard. Spoken cues if you would rather keep the phone in your pocket. Light or dark, whichever suits the room.

Stop early and what you did still counts. Squeezing to fatigue and stopping is correct technique, not a failure.

A diary worth keeping

Leaks, pads, drinks, trips to the toilet. How much, and what you were doing at the time. Three days of honest recording tells a continence nurse more than a month of describing it from memory.

Share a summary and CSV files straight from your phone. You choose where it goes; Anchor has nowhere of its own to send it.

Straight answers

Short pieces on finding the right muscle, what to expect after surgery, the mistakes that quietly stop it working, and an honest read of the evidence — including where that evidence is uncertain.

Every article names its published sources, among them Prostate Cancer UK, Macmillan Cancer Support, NHS trust patient information and the Cochrane Library.

Nothing leaves your phone

No account. No sign-in. No analytics. No cloud. Anchor contains no networking code at all — your exercise record and your diary are on your device and nowhere else.

You can erase all of it in two taps. Read the privacy policy.

Free

All of it. Nothing to unlock, no subscription, no adverts, no upsell.

Important

Anchor is an exercise timer and a diary. It cannot assess you, and it cannot check that you are contracting the right muscle — that needs a men's health physiotherapist.

Contact your clinical team or NHS 111 if you cannot pass urine, see unexpected blood, or develop pain, fever or new weakness.
