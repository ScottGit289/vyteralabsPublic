01

The problem it solves

After prostate surgery you are told to do pelvic floor exercises three times a day, for at least three months.

Almost nobody does. The sheet gets lost, the reminder never comes, and by the time of the follow-up appointment nobody can say what was actually done.

Anchor is a quiet timer, a reminder that arrives, and a record you can hand to your physiotherapist. It does not assess you and it does not treat you. It makes the programme you were given happen.

02

What it does

01

Built for where you are

Before surgery. Catheter still in. Catheter just out. Months down the line. Anchor asks, and sets a starting programme to match. If your catheter is still in it holds the programme — because UK guidance is to start active exercises once it has been removed. Your team decides when, not an app.

02

A set, done discreetly

A calm ring counts you through long squeezes and quick squeezes. Vibration-only cues as standard, spoken cues if you would rather keep the phone in your pocket. Stop early and what you did still counts — squeezing to fatigue and stopping is correct technique, not a failure.

03

A diary worth keeping

Leaks, pads, drinks, trips to the toilet — how much, and what you were doing at the time. Three days of honest recording tells a continence nurse more than a month of describing it from memory. Share a summary and CSV files straight from your phone.

04

Straight answers

Short pieces on finding the right muscle, what to expect after surgery, the mistakes that quietly stop it working, and an honest read of the evidence — including where that evidence is uncertain. Every article names its published sources.

03

Nothing leaves your phone

Data collected None

No account. No sign-in. No analytics. No cloud. Anchor contains no networking code at all — your exercise record and your diary are on your device and nowhere else.

The only way anything leaves is if you tap Share summary and choose where to send it. You pick the destination; Anchor has none of its own.

You can erase everything in two taps. Read the privacy policy.

04

Free, and staying that way

Nothing to unlock, no subscription, no adverts, no upsell. The men who most need this are the least likely to weigh up a paid medical app while coming to terms with a diagnosis. Removing the price removes the last excuse not to start.
