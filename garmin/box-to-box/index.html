The work football
is made of.

Box to Box records football training sessions, small-sided games and 5-a-side, and measures the running that decides them: repeated short sprints, high-intensity distance, and every hard acceleration and deceleration. Not steady jogging. It saves a native Football activity to Garmin Connect, with every metric inside it.

01

Three pages, read at a glance

Mid-drill, you have a second to look. So each page carries one big number and two smaller ones, sized to be read at arm's length on the move. Swipe, or press UP and DOWN, to change page.

Clock: the period clock, total elapsed time and heart rate. Running: distance, max speed and sprint count. Intensity: high-intensity distance, accelerations and decelerations.

At full time the watch buzzes and the clock turns amber, counting added time until you press LAP. The whistle ends the period, not the watch.

02

Five metrics, defined like the professionals'

The defaults follow the thresholds used by professional GPS vests, so the words mean what a coach means by them. Each threshold is adjustable.

Sprints

Runs above 25 km/h, held for at least one second. One run counts once, even if you hover around the line.

High-intensity distance

Distance covered above 19.8 km/h, the high-speed-running line of the professional game.

Accelerations and decelerations

Efforts beyond ±3 m/s². A stoppage is never counted as a deceleration, nor the restart as an acceleration.

Distance, speed and heart

Total distance, max speed, average and maximum heart rate, time in each of your Garmin zones, and calories.

A watch records GPS once a second; a professional vest records ten times a second. Box to Box counts the same efforts by the same definitions, but a burst shorter than a second cannot be seen at one fix a second, so its acceleration counts run lower than a vest's. Compare your sessions with each other, not with a club's vest data.

03

Built for how a session actually runs

Periods. One to four, of five to sixty minutes; the default is two halves of 45. LAP ends a period, and half-time counts towards neither.

Stoppages. START pauses when the coach stops the drill, and the period carries on when you resume.

Extra time. Five, ten or fifteen minutes, added from the menu at any point, including after the final whistle.

A tackle cannot end your session. Screen taps and swipe-back are ignored while a session runs, and leaving is always a deliberate choice from the menu.

04

Training, not match day

Box to Box is for training sessions, small-sided games and 5-a-side. It is not a match-day app, and it makes no claim that you may wear a watch in a match. Under Law 4 of the Laws of the Game the referee decides what players may wear, and competitions can add their own rules on tracking devices.

05

Devices and data

Watches: 71 models on Connect IQ System 7 (API 5.0) or later, the level Garmin requires for paid Connect IQ apps. They include Forerunner 165 to 970, fēnix 7, 8, 9 and E, epix (Gen 2) and epix Pro, Venu 2, 3 and 4, Venu Sq 2, vívoactive 5 and 6, Instinct 3, Instinct E and Instinct Crossover AMOLED, Enduro 3, MARQ (Gen 2), Approach S50 and S70, Descent G2 and Mk3, and D2. The full list is in the user guide. GPS is multi-band where the watch has it, and the best available otherwise.

Permissions: five, each for one job. Fit and FitContributor create the activity and write the football metrics into it. Positioning supplies GPS speed. Sensor supplies heart rate, including from a chest strap. User Profile supplies your heart-rate zones.

Data: no phone, no companion app, no account, no cloud service. Your session goes into the watch's own activity file and reaches Garmin Connect the same way every other activity on that watch does. Nothing is sent to Vytera Labs.
