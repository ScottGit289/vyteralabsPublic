A Vytera Labs watch face · Garmin Connect IQ

Data rich,
and still readable.

ClearSight is built to be vibrant and data rich and still readable by someone whose sight is no longer sharp. Those two goals pull against each other — density costs type size — so it does not pick one for you. It gives you three layouts and four palettes, and sizes everything from your watch’s real screen and fonts.

01

The clock gets what is left

Most faces pick a font and lay out around it. ClearSight measures the data fields, the date and the seconds first, then takes the largest numeric font that fits the space that remains. On a round screen the usable width narrows towards the top and bottom, so where the digits sit changes how wide they may be — and the face measures that too.

Rich

Five data fields around the clock. The default.

Balanced

Three fields — the lower pair and the bottom one — and a much larger clock.

Giant clock

The time and date, as large as the glass allows, with only the bottom field kept — set it to Off for the clock alone.

Ring off

Turn the progress ring off and the band it used goes to the clock, which grows again.

02

Four palettes, each for a condition

Neon Night

Bright accents on true black. Maximum vibrancy, no glare. The default.

Max Contrast

Black, white and yellow only. The strongest option for advanced low vision, and unaffected by any form of colour blindness.

Daylight Bold

Dark ink on white, for bright sun and for eyes that see light-on-dark text smear.

Amber Night

Warm amber on black, free of blue light, and the least disruptive at night.

03

Readable by design

Every field is labelled twice — by a solid icon and by a colour — so neither colour blindness nor a washed-out screen loses information.

Nothing is drawn thin. Icons are filled silhouettes, not outlines, and the progress ring reads as a band rather than a hairline.

Values never reflow. Each field is sized against the widest number it can ever show, so the step count does not shrink when it passes ten thousand.

Numbers over four digits are grouped. 8,432, not 8432.

04

Fields and ring

Five slots, each free to show heart rate, steps, battery percent, battery days remaining, calories, distance, floors climbed, active minutes for today or the week, Body Battery, stress, temperature, notifications, or step-goal percent.

A progress ring around the edge tracks your step goal, battery, floors or weekly active minutes, and turns green the moment the goal is met.

Settings: select the face on your watch and choose Customize, beside Apply, to set everything on the watch itself. The same choices are in Garmin Connect, under Connect IQ → ClearSight → Settings.

05

Devices and data

Watches: 69 models on Connect IQ System 7 — Approach S50 and S70; D2 Air X10, Mach 1 and Mach 2; Descent G2 and Mk3; Enduro 3; epix (Gen 2) and epix Pro; fēnix 7, 7 Pro, 7S, 7X, 8, 8 Pro, 8 Solar, E and 9 families; Forerunner 70, 165, 170, 255, 265, 570, 955, 965 and 970 families; Instinct 3 AMOLED and Instinct Crossover AMOLED; MARQ (Gen 2) and MARQ Aviator (Gen 2); Venu 2, 2 Plus, 2S, 3, 3S, 4, Sq 2 and X1; vívoactive 5 and 6. Each was run in the simulator and its screen checked. The user guide lists the watches left out, and why.

Permissions: one. Sensor History — Reads the watch’s own recorded history to fill the optional Body Battery, stress and temperature fields. It is read on the watch, for display on the watch.

Data: the face reads what your watch already knows and displays it. There is no network code, no account, and nothing is sent anywhere. Your settings live in the watch’s own storage.

Price: one payment of £1.99 in the UK, $1.99 in the US. No subscription, no trial, no in-app purchase. Garmin’s 48-hour refund window applies.
