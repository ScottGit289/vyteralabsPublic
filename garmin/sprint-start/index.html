01

Why the holds are random

The point of a start drill is that the athlete cannot anticipate the gun. Two things follow from that, and both are deliberate.

The holds are drawn to the millisecond, not the whole second. On Your Marks and Set each last a random length inside the range you choose. With only a handful of whole-second outcomes an athlete quickly learns to count the hold and time the drive out of the blocks against it. Milliseconds make that impossible.

No countdown is shown during a random stage. A visible countdown would hand back exactly the information the randomness exists to hide. The only countdown on screen is during Get Ready, and only while that stage is set to a fixed length.

The stopwatch zero is taken at the instant the Go cue is issued — so the time includes the athlete's reaction to the sound they actually heard, not to a signal the watch sent early.

02

The sequence

Four commands, each with its own cue. Get Ready is a single beep and a buzz, and holds for a fixed ten seconds by default. On Your Marks and Set are the random ones — the second of them doubled, two beeps rather than one, because Set is the command an athlete must never mistake for another and a doubled cue carries to the far end of a track when a single one does not. Go is one beep, and the clock is already running.

Exact cues and default hold ranges are in the user guide.

03

Devices and data

Watches: Forerunner 265 and Forerunner 265S, on Connect IQ System 3.2 or later. More devices can be added — if you want yours supported, say which model it is.

Permissions: none. The app declares no permissions at all. Tones and vibration require none, and it asks for nothing else.

Data: no GPS, no internet, no account, no activity recorded, nothing collected. Your start times exist on the screen until you reset them, and nowhere else.
