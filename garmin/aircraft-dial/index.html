An analogue face with a screwed bezel, a full 60-tick ring, a luminous twelve o'clock index, three sub-dials and a rectangular readout where an altimeter puts its Kollsman window. Every sub-dial and that window is a data field you choose.

01

The face

Four themes, four configurable slots, and a second hand you can put in a sub-dial, sweep from the centre, or switch off entirely.

02

What sits where

Top sub-dial

The seconds sweep by default. Set the second hand elsewhere and the slot becomes a gauge field of your choosing — battery, body battery, anything with a range.

Left and right sub-dials

A needle and a value. Steps on the left and heart rate on the right by default.

Bottom window

A label and a value in the Kollsman position. Date by default; barometric pressure is the one it was designed around.

The needle means something

Fields with a natural range — battery, steps and floors against their goals, heart rate against your zones, body battery, stress — drive the needle across a 270° sweep. The rest show a value with the needle hidden.

Fields whose sensor your watch does not have read -- rather than an invented number.

03

Instructions for use

Applying the face

Open the watch face list on your watch, scroll to Aircraft Dial, and choose Apply.

Changing what it shows

There are two ways in, and both edit the same settings:

  • On the watch — select the face and choose Customize, the option beside Apply. Changes apply immediately.
  • From the phone — Garmin Connect → your device → Watch Face Settings.
If you sideloaded the face, use Customize on the watch. Garmin Connect can only edit settings for apps installed from the store, so a sideloaded face appears in the app count but cannot be opened from the phone. Customize does not involve the store. Older watches without on-device settings support show only Apply, and are limited to the phone route.

The data fields you can choose from

Off, Date, Battery, Steps, Heart Rate, Calories, Distance, Floors Climbed, Active Minutes, Body Battery, Stress, Altitude, Barometric Pressure, Temperature, Notifications, and Time to Recovery.

Two behaviours worth knowing

The Top Sub-dial setting is ignored while the second hand is in a sub-dial mode, because the seconds sweep occupies that slot. The on-device menu shows that row as "Seconds sub-dial" rather than offering a field that would have no effect. Set the second hand to Off or Centre sweep and the slot becomes yours.

Asleep, the seconds needle parks at twelve rather than freezing wherever the once-a-minute redraw happened to land.

Battery, and how to spend it

The cost of the face is driven by its redraw schedule, not by how many data fields you switch on — an unused sensor is never opened at all.

StateWork done
Asleep · seconds Off or wrist-upOne full redraw per minute.
Asleep · burn-in display (AMOLED)One minimal, pixel-shifted redraw per minute.
Asleep · seconds Always onThe above, plus one small clipped repaint per second.
Wrist up · any second handOne full redraw per second, while the wrist is up.

Second Hand → Off is the cheapest setting. Always on is the only one that costs anything while the watch is asleep — and if your watch reports that the per-second repaint is too expensive, the face drops itself back to wrist-up rather than letting the system freeze it.

On watches that need burn-in protection

Asleep on an AMOLED watch, the face draws outline-only hands, four ticks and nothing else, drifting inside a five-pixel box so no pixel is held lit.

04

Built to be cheap to draw

No runtime trigonometry. Every angle the face uses lands on a whole degree, so a 360-entry fixed-point sine table built once at startup replaces every sine call. Hand, tick and needle geometry is integer arithmetic throughout.

No allocation in the draw path. The polygon buffer, the clip rectangle and the reading objects are allocated once and reused.

Data sampled once a minute, not once a draw. However often the face repaints, the activity and sensor sources are read at most once per minute — and only if a slot is actually configured to use them.

Tight partial updates. The always-on second hand repaints only the top sub-dial's bounding box: roughly two dozen primitives inside a small rectangle, rather than a full-screen redraw.

One resource tree. All geometry is derived from the device's screen size at runtime, so there are no per-resolution layouts and no bitmaps to load beyond the launcher icon. A new watch needs no new artwork.

05

Devices and data

Watches: 28 models on Connect IQ 3.2 or later — epix (Gen 2); fēnix 6, 6S, 6 Pro, 6S Pro and 6X Pro; fēnix 7, 7S and 7X; Forerunner 255, 255S, 265, 265S, 745, 945, 955 and 965; Venu, Venu 2, Venu 2S, Venu 2 Plus, Venu 3, Venu 3S, Venu Sq and Venu Sq 2; vívoactive 4, 4S and 5.

Permissions: two. Sensor History backs the optional altitude, barometric pressure, temperature, Body Battery and Stress fields. User Profile supplies the resting rate and zone ceiling that scale the heart-rate needle — without it, that gauge would need an invented range. Steps, calories and the rest need no permission at all.

Data: the face reads what your watch already knows and displays it. There is no network code, no account, and nothing is sent anywhere. Your settings live in the watch's own storage.
