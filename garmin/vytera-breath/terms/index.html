3. Not medical advice, and not a game

Vytera Breath is a training tool. It is not a medical device, it does not diagnose or treat anything, and nothing it displays is medical advice.

Never practise breath retention in or near water. Shallow-water blackout gives no warning and happens to strong swimmers. Never practise while driving or operating anything. Sit or lie down — never stand, because a faint while standing is a head injury rather than a moment of dizziness.

Speak to a doctor before using breath-hold or hyperventilation protocols if you are pregnant, or if you have a cardiovascular condition, high or low blood pressure, a history of fainting, epilepsy or any seizure disorder, a respiratory condition including asthma or COPD, a history of stroke or aneurysm, or if you are recovering from surgery.

Stop immediately if you feel faint, unwell, numb, or in pain at any point.

Breathing protocols can produce dizziness, tingling in the hands and face, and muscle tightening. These are expected effects of low carbon dioxide and pass. Losing consciousness is not an expected effect: if it happens, stop and seek medical advice before practising again.

The app will never prescribe a hold longer than three minutes, and it watches for an unusually low heart rate and for losing your pulse during a hold. Those safeguards reduce risk; they do not remove it, and they are no substitute for your own judgement. Heart rate from a wrist optical sensor is an estimate and can be wrong or absent.

The App asks you to acknowledge these warnings twice: once on first launch, and once more — separately — before your first breath-hold. Accepting either is a confirmation that you have read this section and judged the protocols appropriate for you. It is not a transfer of that judgement to us.

You are solely responsible for deciding whether these protocols are appropriate for you, and for the conditions you practise in.
