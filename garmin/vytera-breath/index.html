01

Before anything else

Breath retention is not a game.
  • Never practise breath retention in or near water. Shallow-water blackout gives no warning and happens to strong swimmers.
  • Never practise while driving, or while operating anything.
  • Sit or lie down — never stand. A faint while standing is a head injury rather than a moment of dizziness.

The app asks you to acknowledge those three rules before your first session. It asks again — separately, once — before your first breath-hold, because the risks that matter for a hold are not the risks of box breathing and you should not have to remember a warning you accepted months earlier. It will never prescribe a hold longer than three minutes, whatever you manage. It watches for an unusually low heart rate and for losing your pulse during a hold, and tells you to check in if it sees either.

This is a training tool, not medical advice, and it diagnoses and treats nothing. Speak to a doctor first if you are pregnant, or have a cardiovascular condition, high or low blood pressure, a history of fainting, epilepsy or any seizure disorder, a respiratory condition including asthma or COPD, a history of stroke or aneurysm, or if you are recovering from surgery. The full contraindications are in the user guide and in the terms.

02

Guided by feel, not by screen

Every phase of every protocol is carried by vibration, and the patterns were designed so you never have to count or remember which is which.

Direction you can feel

The inhale cue rises in intensity; the exhale cue falls. You do not have to decode the pattern — it moves the way the breath moves.

A hold feels different

A hold is categorically different from a breath, so it gets a categorically different shape: staccato taps rather than a ramp, with a quiet pulse to say the app is still there.

One pattern you cannot miss

The check-in alert is the only long–short–long pattern in the app, and the only one that ignores your intensity setting. Turn vibration down to light and you will still feel that one.

Screen off entirely

Vibration-only mode stops the app asking for redraws at all. Eyes closed, wrist down, nothing to look at.

03

Eight protocols

From a ninety-second pre-race reset to a twenty-minute resonance session. Each one is configurable, and each starts from a deliberately conservative default.

Cyclic breathing with retention — paced breaths, then a hold. CO2 tolerance table — the same hold with shrinking rest, or a growing hold with fixed rest. Box breathing — equal in, hold, out, hold, three to eight seconds a phase. Resonance — slow breathing from 4.5 to 7 a minute. Physiological sigh — double inhale, long exhale. Extended exhale — an exhale twice as long as the inhale. Pre-race reset — wake up, then settle. And a custom builder, if none of those is what you want.

Every protocol's defaults and adjustable range are listed in the user guide.

04

Measured, not just timed

Your watch already measures the thing these protocols are trying to change. This app uses it during the session.

Let your heart rate end the hold

Optionally, a hold ends when your heart rate climbs eight beats above its lowest point in that hold — the rise that comes with the urge to breathe — rather than at a number you picked before you started.

A coherence band, not a score

For resonance and box breathing it reads beat-to-beat variability where your watch exposes it, and shows three bands. A wrist sensor cannot honestly support more precision than that, and we would rather say so.

How well you came back down

At the end, in plain words: how far your heart rate fell from where it started, and how quickly it returned. Two components, each explainable in a sentence.

Targets from your own sessions

Retention targets are the rolling median of your last five holds, stepped up only when the last two sessions both ended without a heart-rate spike. Progression follows calm sessions, not heroic ones.

Where a number cannot be trusted it is not shown. If your watch exposes neither a variability figure nor beat-to-beat intervals, the coherence indicator reads -- and the screen says so rather than inventing something. A missing heart rate never ends a hold early either — losing the sensor should not be indistinguishable from succeeding.

05

In Garmin Connect

Every saved session becomes a breathwork activity — recorded as meditation with a breathing sub-sport — so your watch and Garmin Connect show a summary that fits, rather than a generic one with average speed and distance in it. It syncs the way every other activity does. Duration, the heart-rate graph, and one lap per round — so hold times read straight off the lap table rather than needing anything custom to be rendered.

On top of that, developer fields graph the breathing phase, the breath rate, variability and the coherence band. Recording keeps running for up to two minutes after your last breath, so the recovery is on the graph too.

Heart-rate data from saved sessions feeds your watch's own Body Battery and stress metrics exactly as any recorded activity does. This app cannot read those scores and cannot change them — and anyone who tells you otherwise about any app is guessing.

06

Devices and data

Watches: 41 models on Connect IQ System 6 (API 4.2) or later — the Forerunner 165, 255, 265, 570, 955, 965 and 970 families; fēnix 7, 7 Pro and 8; epix (Gen 2) and epix Pro; Enduro 3; Instinct 3 in AMOLED and Solar; Venu 2, 3 and X1; and vívoactive 5 and 6. Round, rectangular and high-contrast monochrome layouts are all drawn from the screen at runtime.

Permissions: four. Fit records the session as an activity with its laps. FIT Contributor writes the breathing fields into that activity — a separate right from recording it. Sensor supplies live heart rate and, where the watch exposes them, beat-to-beat intervals. User Profile is read once, only to seed a sensible default for the low-heart-rate check-in.

Data: no location, no network, no account, no companion app. Nothing leaves the watch except the activity you save, which goes to your own Garmin Connect account by Garmin's own sync. Nothing is sent to Vytera Labs.
