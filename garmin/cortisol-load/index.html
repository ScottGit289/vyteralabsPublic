How far from your normal
is your body today?

Cortisol Load takes the stress and recovery signals your Garmin watch already measures — resting heart rate, stress, Body Battery, training load, sleep score where the watch has it, and an optional two-minute HRV check — and scores them against your own baseline, not a population average. One number from 0 to 100, where 50 is your normal. It is an estimate of physiological stress. It does not measure cortisol.

01

Measured against you, not against everyone

A resting heart rate of 58 is ordinary for one person and a warning sign for another. So Cortisol Load never compares you with a population. Each signal is compared with your own rolling baseline — 28 days by default — using the median and a robust spread, so one unusual day cannot move it.

The score is the confidence-weighted average of whichever signals your watch genuinely provides today. 50 is your normal. Above 60 means your signals, taken together, sit on the stressed side of your baseline; below 40, on the recovered side.

Score and state

0–100, shown as Very Low, Low, Moderate, High or Very High, with the change against your 7-day average.

Confidence

From Building baseline to High, based on how many days of data you have and how many signals are present. It never claims more than the data supports.

Contributors

Which signals moved and which way: HRV down, resting heart rate up, sleep down. Tap through for the plain-language explanation.

Trend

The last 7 or 28 days against your own reference level. The axis carries no units, because the score is an estimate, not a medical quantity.

02

The signals, and the honest gaps

Connect IQ apps can read far less than Garmin Connect shows on your phone. Cortisol Load uses exactly what a watch app is allowed to read, and says so when something is missing.

SignalUsed?
Resting heart rateYes — Garmin's daily value.
StressYes — your 24-hour all-day stress average.
Body BatteryYes — the 24-hour peak, usually your post-sleep level.
Training loadYes — recent active minutes against your usual, plus Garmin's recovery time.
Sleep scoreWhere your watch lets apps read it. Every Garmin watch shows you a sleep score, but only newer firmware — Connect IQ API 6.0.2, such as fēnix 8, Forerunner 570 and 970, Venu 4, vívoactive 6 and Enduro 3 — makes it available to apps. Elsewhere it is used automatically once a firmware update adds it.
HRVThrough a two-minute seated check you start from the menu. Garmin's overnight HRV and HRV Status are not available to apps.
RespirationOptional, in experimental mode.
Body temperatureNo — not available to Connect IQ apps.

A missing signal is never filled in with a guess. The score is built from what is genuinely there, and the screen says so: Based on 4 of 5 signals. For the first seven days it shows Baseline building and no number at all.

03

What it is not

It does not measure cortisol. No watch can. Cortisol Load is inspired by the link between autonomic activity — the balance your heart-rate signals reflect — and the body's stress response, and it is named for that idea. It does not estimate a cortisol level, it is not a medical device, and it is for information only. It does not diagnose, treat or predict any condition.

Training is not the enemy. Hard training raises the score, as it should — exercise is a stressor, and a healthy one. The app labels it Training load, separately from the physiological signals, and never tells you to stop exercising.

If your score stays high for days, the explanation says what the evidence supports and no more: persistent changes can have many causes — sleep, training, hydration, alcohol, illness — and if you are concerned about how you feel, speak with a healthcare professional.

04

Devices and data

Watches: 51 round-screen models on Connect IQ API 5.0 or later, including Forerunner 165 to 970, fēnix 7, 8 and E, epix (Gen 2) and epix Pro, Venu 2, 3 and 4, vívoactive 5 and 6, Enduro 3, Instinct 3 AMOLED, MARQ (Gen 2), Approach S50 and S70, Descent G2 and Mk3, and D2. The full list is in the user guide.

Permissions: three, each for one job. Sensor History reads your stress and Body Battery history. Sensor reads beat-to-beat intervals, only during the HRV check you start. User Profile reads Garmin's resting heart rate.

Data: no phone, no companion app, no account, no cloud, no network permission. Up to 90 days of daily values stay on the watch, and you can delete them from the menu at any time. Nothing is sent to Vytera Labs.
