01

The dial

A rotating dial with a fixed needle, or a fixed dial with a rotating needle — your choice. Cardinal points, 30° ticks, a lubber line at the top, and the heading in numerals large enough to read at arm's length. The needle is filtered so it settles instead of twitching, with three smoothing levels.

Lock a bearing

Press to lock the bearing you are facing. The dial shows how far left or right you have drifted, and buzzes when you come back within three degrees. One more press gives you the back bearing for the return leg.

Wind on the dial

The wind direction is drawn on the compass itself and turns as you turn. A headwind sits behind you on the dial, a tailwind ahead — so you can see what you are walking, running or riding into.

Sun on the dial

The sun's current bearing and elevation, marked on the dial. It doubles as a backup: if the magnetometer will not calibrate, the app still tells you where the sun is, so you can orient yourself the old way.

Weather you already have

Condition, temperature and feels-like, wind, humidity, precipitation, pressure and its trend, and the next three hours. Every reading is dated, and old data is labelled old.

02

Honest about north

A compass that displays TRUE while being seventeen degrees out — and pointing the wrong side of true north — is worse than one that only offers magnetic, because you have no way to know. So the app tells you which north it is giving you, on the dial, at all times.

Magnetic north is always available and always correct. For true north, enter the declination printed on your map — Ordnance Survey and most national sheets carry it — and the app applies it. Automatic, computed true north unlocks only where a full magnetic model is installed on the watch; until then the app refuses to offer it rather than quietly showing you a number it cannot stand behind.

The same rule runs through the app: a value that cannot be trusted is shown as a dash. Nothing is estimated into looking like a measurement.

03

Devices and data

Devices: Connect IQ System 6 (API 4.2) or later, in three families — round watches, the high-contrast Instinct display, and Edge cycling computers in a landscape layout. The final device list is fixed at release and will be listed here and on the store page.

Permissions: three. Sensor for the magnetometer and the barometer, Sensor History for the pressure trend, and Positioning — which stays off until you switch position features on yourself.

Nothing is recorded. No activity, no FIT file, no track, no timeline entry. The locked bearing and your settings live in the watch's own storage and nowhere else.

No network. The app does not request the Communications permission. It cannot send anything anywhere, and does not.
