Privacy Policy

Publisher: Vytera Labs Ltd  ·  Effective: 4 September 2026  ·  info@vyteralabs.com

Vytera Labs builds Cairn to be private by design. Cairn does not collect any data. It cannot. The app contains no networking code, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifier and no third-party SDKs of any kind.

The short version

  • Cairn is local-only — your to-dos are stored on your device and nowhere else.
  • There is no account system, no analytics or tracking SDKs, and no advertising SDKs.
  • We cannot see your data. There is nothing for us to sell, share, or lose.

What is stored, and where

WhatWhereWho can read it
Your to-dos, projects, areas, tags and checklists A database inside the app's sandbox on your device Only you, on that device
Your preferences (capacity, buffer, display options, review tone) The app's UserDefaults, in the same sandbox Only you, on that device
A snapshot of today's list for the home-screen widget A shared App Group folder on your device Only the app and its own widget, on that device
Reminders Local notifications scheduled by your device Only you

All of it is protected by your device passcode and included in your normal encrypted device backup if you use one.

What Cairn does not do

  • No account, sign-in or registration.
  • No sync — not to us, not to iCloud, not to anyone.
  • No analytics or telemetry of any kind.
  • No crash reporting.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifier, no tracking.
  • No third-party libraries or SDKs.
  • No network requests. The app makes none, at any time, for any reason.

Apple Intelligence

On iOS 26 and later, on a device with Apple Intelligence enabled, three optional features use Apple's on-device Foundation Models framework:

  1. tidying up a rough captured note;
  2. suggesting next steps for a project;
  3. writing the opening paragraph of the weekly review.

These run on your device using a model that ships with iOS. Nothing is sent to Apple, to us, or to anyone else. All three are optional, produce suggestions you choose to accept, and are unnecessary — the app is fully functional without them, and they can be switched off in Settings → Use Apple Intelligence.

Search additionally uses the Natural Language framework's sentence embeddings, which likewise ship with iOS and run locally.

Your calendar

If you switch on Plan around my calendar, Cairn asks iOS for calendar access and reads your events so it can subtract booked time from your planning capacity.

  • It is off until you turn it on. Cairn never asks on launch.
  • It only reads. Cairn calls exactly two calendar APIs — one to ask for access, one to fetch events for a date range. There is no write, edit or delete call anywhere in the app.
  • Events are held in memory for the current session and are never copied into Cairn's database, never exported, and never sent anywhere.
  • Revoke it at any time in iOS Settings → Cairn → Calendars, or by switching the toggle off. Everything else keeps working.

Why iOS asks for "Full Access"

Because iOS does not offer a read-only choice. Since iOS 17 an app can hold one of exactly three calendar states:

iOS choiceWhat it actually grants
NoneNothing.
Add Events OnlyWrite without read: the app can create events it can never read back.
Full AccessRead and write.

There is no fourth "read only" option to pick. Reading your meetings requires Full Access, so Full Access is what Cairn has to ask for — and "Add Events Only" is the worst choice for Cairn, because it hands over the one power Cairn never uses and withholds the only one it needs.

What stops Cairn writing is therefore the code, not the permission: the app contains no calendar-write call to run. Cairn also does not declare NSCalendarsWriteOnlyAccessUsageDescription in its Info.plist, so iOS would not permit it to write even if such a call existed.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Cairn schedules local reminders for the to-dos you asked to be reminded about. These are created by your device, delivered by your device, and never leave it. You may decline; everything else keeps working.

Siri, Shortcuts and the widget

Cairn offers App Intents so you can add a to-do or check today's plan by voice or from a Shortcut. These are handled by iOS on your device. The home-screen widget reads a small snapshot of today's list from an App Group folder shared between the app and its own widget on the same device. Neither leaves the device.

Your data is yours

Settings → Export a backup writes a plain, readable, versioned JSON file containing everything. Export as Markdown writes a readable outline. You can restore either onto any device running Cairn, and you can open both in a text editor.

We believe local-only storage is only a promise worth making if you can always get your data out, so getting it out is a two-tap operation with no format lock-in.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything. There is nothing left anywhere else, because there was never anything anywhere else. Settings → Start fresh achieves the same without deleting the app.

Your rights under UK GDPR

Rights of access, rectification, erasure, portability and objection all concern personal data held by a controller. Vytera Labs Ltd holds no personal data from Cairn: we never receive any, so there is nothing for us to disclose, correct, erase or transfer.

In practice you exercise every one of those rights on your own device — export gives you portability, editing gives you rectification, and deleting the app gives you erasure. If you email us, we hold that email in order to reply to you, and nothing more.

Children

Cairn is rated 4+. It collects nothing from anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Cairn ever gains a network feature, this policy will be updated before that version ships, and the change will be described in the release notes rather than buried here.
