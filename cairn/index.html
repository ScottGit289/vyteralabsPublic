Cairn pulls out the deadline, the estimate, the project, the tag and the priority, and shows you every one as a chip before you save. Nothing is guessed behind your back. It understands today, tonight, tomorrow, next Tuesday, this weekend, end of month, in three days, in a fortnight, every other Monday, and a great deal more.

Crucially, it separates a deadline from a plan. "Tomorrow" is a promise to yourself. "By Friday" is a promise to somebody else. Only one of those should survive a busy week, and Cairn knows which.

02

Plan My Day

Set your real working time and a buffer for the unexpected. Cairn protects your deadlines, ranks everything else on urgency, priority, age and project pressure, puts deep work in a protected block before the admin, stops any single area swallowing the whole day — and then tells you exactly what it held back and why.

It is arithmetic, not a guess. Same inputs, same plan, every time, and every line explains itself. Switch on Plan around my calendar and booked meetings come out of your capacity first, because a day plan that ignores four hours of meetings is not a plan, it is a wish.

04

The weekly review, done for you

Finished versus last week. Inbox backing up. Deadlines already blown. Projects going nowhere. To-dos older than a month. One area eating your life. Every finding is computed from your own data and taps straight through to the thing it is about.

You also choose how it talks to you — Supportive, Encouraging, Friendly, Just the facts, or Direct.

The tone changes the wording and nothing else. The figures and findings are identical in all five, because a review that could flatter you by choosing kinder arithmetic would be worthless.

And every tone, Direct included, is held to the same floor: blunt about the work, never about you. Cairn will tell you nothing was finished this week. It will not tell you that you are a failure — it is a to-do list working from nine numbers, and it has no idea whether you spent the week in hospital.

05

On-device intelligence, stated precisely

01

Cairn's own engine

The capture parser, focus scorer, day planner, review engine and semantic search are written in Swift and run locally on every supported device, offline, with no model in the loop. That is the whole app, and it never depends on anything else.

02

Apple's on-device model

On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, Cairn additionally uses Apple's on-device model for exactly three things: tidying a rambling note, breaking a project into next actions, and writing the weekly review paragraph. Each is optional, produces a suggestion you accept or reject, and can be switched off entirely.

03

There is no layer three

Nothing from your library is sent anywhere, because there is nowhere to send it. Cairn has no server, no account, and makes no network requests of its own — the one exception being Apple's rating prompt, which carries a star and nothing elsd.

06

What Cairn will never do

  • No account, sign-in or registration.
  • No sync — not to us, not to iCloud, not to anyone.
  • No analytics or telemetry of any kind.
  • No crash reporting.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifier, no tracking.
  • No third-party libraries or SDKs.
  • No network requests. Cairn opens no connection of its own — the one exception is Apple's rating prompt, drawn by iOS and answered to Apple, never to us. Set out in full in the privacy policy.
  • No subscription and no in-app purchases. You buy it once.

Local-only is a promise worth making only if you can always get your data out — so Export a backup writes plain, readable, versioned JSON, and Export as Markdown writes a readable outline. Both open in a text editor. There is no format lock-in, because there is nothing to lock you into.

Read the full privacy policy — including exactly why iOS asks for calendar "Full Access" when Cairn only ever reads — or the complete user guide.

07

Everything else, and what it needs

Deadlines, reminders, repeating to-dos, checklists, tags, areas, project sections, effort estimates, energy levels, a home-screen widget, Siri and Shortcuts, full VoiceOver support, Dynamic Type, light and dark themes, and a complete user guide — inside the app and on this site.

Waiting-on dependencies are enforced, not decorated. A to-do waiting on unfinished work is kept out of your day plan and cannot be ticked done — tap its padlock and Cairn names what it is waiting on. A rule the app states and does not enforce would teach you not to trust the ones it does.

Capture inherits where you are standing. Add a to-do from Someday and it is parked in Someday; from a project or an area it is filed there; from Today it is dated today. The row above the field says where it will land and why, before you commit — and anything you type overrules it.

£5.99, once, and then it is yours. One purchase, not a subscription. No in-app purchases, no feature held back behind a paid tier, no upgrade price at the next major version, and nothing that stops working if you never open the App Store again. That is not generosity — it is the only pricing model consistent with an app that makes no network requests, because charging you monthly would mean shipping code that keeps phoning Apple to check you are still paying.

Nothing is deleted unless you ask. Finished work is kept forever by default. Choose a window in Settings if you would rather the Logbook stayed navigable, and Cairn quotes exactly how much will go before it goes.
