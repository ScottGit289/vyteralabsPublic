Longevity Counter

User Guide

Publisher: Vytera Labs Ltd  ·  For: Longevity Counter on iPhone and iPad  ·  support@vyteralabs.com

Longevity Counter turns your health numbers into an evidence-based estimate of how long — and how healthily — you are likely to live, and ranks what is worth changing. This guide explains each screen, how to feed it good data, and what the model can and cannot say.

Read this first
  • The app never predicts a date of death. It estimates population-level probabilities of reaching given ages.
  • It is an evidence-assembled model, not a prospectively validated clinical risk score. Treat the outputs as calibrated illustrations.
  • It never claims an intervention "adds N years" — it reports a model-implied shift, always labelled as such.
  • Your data stays on your device. Nothing is uploaded to Vytera Labs; there are no accounts and no tracking.
  • Always consult a qualified clinician about health decisions. See the medical disclaimer.

2. Getting started

There is no sign-in and no account. Open the app and enter what you know.

You do not need every metric, but the estimate is only as good as its inputs. The highest-value numbers to get right early are blood pressure, smoking status, HbA1c or glucose, ApoB or LDL cholesterol, and a measure of fitness such as VO₂max — these carry the most weight in the published evidence behind the model.

If a score shows no data, that whole area is missing. Adding any one of its inputs will light it up.

If you have recent blood results from your GP or a private panel, enter them. A value you type is used exactly as entered.

3. The Home counter and widgets

Your estimated remaining time appears on the Home screen, and as a Home-Screen or Lock-Screen widget.

It is a running expression of the same forecast the rest of the app produces — not a countdown to an event. When your inputs improve, it moves.

4. Lifespan, healthspan and survival odds

The Forecast tab reports:

  • Estimated lifespan, with a likely range rather than a single false-precision figure.
  • Healthspan — lifespan minus an estimated morbidity gap, anchored on the WHO/GBD health-expectancy gap of roughly the last 8–12 years. It is widened by chronic-disease burden and unfavourable modifiable factors, and compressed by high fitness. It is an estimate, explicitly not a validated disability-free-life-expectancy assay.
  • Survival odds — your probability of reaching 70, 75, 80, 85, 90, 95 and 100.

The tab also carries a "How these are calculated & what moves them" screen. If a number surprises you, start there.

5. Your longevity age

Longevity age is the age your risk profile actually resembles, against your chronological age. The app shows the gap in years, a plain summary, and two explicit lists: what lowered it and what raised it.

Like healthspan, it is a communication device rather than a validated assay — useful for seeing direction of travel, not for putting on a form.

7. Risk factors and interventions

Each risk factor is shown with its hazard ratio, whether it is protective, harmful or neutral, whether it is modifiable, your current value, a reference value, a plain interpretation, an evidence level (high, moderate or weak) and the study it comes from.

That last part matters: if you want to challenge a number, the app gives you what you need to do it.

For interventions, the model is recomputed with only that one factor optimised, and the results are ranked by the hazard reduction that factor itself provides. Each shows your current value, an evidence-based target, and the model-implied shift in survival probability and median lifespan.

The app deliberately never says an intervention "adds N years", because this composite is not a single formally validated intervention model. Treat the ranking as the useful output — it tells you where the leverage is.

8. Scenario modelling

Three scenarios are computed by the model, never hard-coded, each recomputing the full survival curve:

ScenarioWhat it assumes
CurrentYour profile exactly as entered.
OptimisedModifiable factors moved toward evidence-based targets. It can never worsen an already-excellent value — a very fit person keeps their high VO₂max.
DecliningModifiable factors deteriorate. It does not fabricate implausible collapses such as a smoking relapse.

The relative difference between scenarios is more trustworthy than the absolute figure in any one of them.

9. Connecting Apple Health and Oura

Apple Health and Oura are supported today; Garmin is planned. Both are connected on the Health tab.

Apple Health

Health tab → Apple Health → grant read access, then Sync. Access is read-only, and you choose which categories to share — the app works with whatever you allow, and anything else can be typed by hand. This needs a real device with Health data on it.

Settings → What Longevity reads from Apple Health lists exactly what it asks for, and Review permissions in the Health app takes you straight to Apple's own controls.

Oura

Health tab → Oura. You sign in on Oura's own secure page, so the app never sees your Oura password. The tokens are stored in your device Keychain and used only to read your Oura data — sleep, HRV and resting heart rate. Your data goes directly from Oura's servers to your device; Vytera Labs never receives or proxies it.

To disconnect: Health tab → Oura → Disconnect, which deletes the stored tokens.

Averages, not single readings

Wearable data is read as 30-, 90- and 365-day windows, with 90-day preferred, because averaged data is far more stable than one reading. One bad night's sleep should not move a lifespan estimate.

A value you type is used exactly as entered and overrides any wearable average for that metric until the next sync — which is what you want when you have a proper clinical measurement.

10. Data quality and confidence

The app scores its own confidence in your forecast, from 0–100, banded:

BandScore
High75 and above
Moderate50 to 74
LowBelow 50

It is built from a weighted checklist of high-value inputs plus a bonus for averaged or windowed data, and it comes with a plain-language explanation naming the specific gaps. If your band is low, that list is your to-do list — blood pressure in particular expects repeated measurements rather than one reading.

Use Settings → Recalculate now to force a fresh run after entering new data.

11. How the model works

It is a proportional-hazards survival model. It starts from an age- and sex-specific mortality baseline and adjusts it using published, all-cause hazard ratios from large cohort studies and meta-analyses — blood pressure, smoking, diabetes and HbA1c, ApoB and cholesterol, fitness and VO₂max, adiposity, activity, alcohol, kidney disease and more.

Two design choices are worth knowing:

  • Correlated inputs are clustered so they are not double-counted. Entering four measures of the same underlying thing does not multiply its effect.
  • Consumer-wearable signals such as HRV are deliberately down-weighted, because their measurement quality does not justify the weight a naive model would give them.

Every component shows its source, study, population, effect size and evidence strength. Settings → The science & sources has the full reference list, and the model version is bumped whenever any coefficient, life table, combination rule or output definition changes.

12. Limitations — please read

  • The baseline life table uses approximate SSA 2021 anchors.
  • Hazard ratios are applied as all-cause and independent after clustering. Real interactions and partial mediation are only approximately handled — lipids, for example, are attenuated because much of their effect runs through cardiovascular disease.
  • The composite is an evidence-assembled model, not a single prospectively validated risk score.
  • Coefficients are drawn largely from higher-income-country cohorts and may not transport perfectly to all populations.
  • Longevity age and healthspan are communication devices, not validated assays.

It cannot predict your exact lifespan, it never predicts a date of death, and it is not a diagnosis or a substitute for professional care. See the medical disclaimer.
