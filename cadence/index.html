01

Two numbers, two jobs

Most trackers reward you with a streak and then punish you with it. Miss one day and months of work reads as zero. That single design choice is why so many people quit. Cadence fixes it without letting you off the hook.

47

Streak

Consecutive days, counted honestly, because it works. It is the number that keeps you moving.

82/100

Momentum

Time-weighted consistency, half-life a fortnight. One missed day costs a few points, not everything, and it climbs back the moment you resume.

Neither number is allowed to do the other's job.

02

Grace days that don't lie

Give a habit one or two grace days a month and a single miss won't end the run. The missed day still shows as missed in your history, and it still counts as a zero against your momentum.

Grace protects your motivation, never your data. A tracker that quietly rewrites the past is a tracker you stop believing.

Pausing works properly too. Holiday, injury, illness — pause a habit and those days are neither wins nor losses.

03

A weekly review, written on your iPhone

Cadence reads your last fortnight and writes a short, specific review: what is working, what is slipping, the one habit worth concentrating on, and a concrete experiment to run for seven days.

It names habits and it names days. It will tell you that Thursday is where your training falls over, or that your reading always happens on days you walk.

On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, Apple's on-device model writes it. On every other iPhone, Cadence's own analysis engine does — so the feature never simply disappears. Either way, every number it quotes is computed by the app from your own logged days. The model is asked to phrase facts, never to work them out.

In your first week it will not pretend to find trends that don't exist yet. It coaches your setup instead, and starts scoring once there is a week behind you.

04

Habits that tick themselves off

Type “gym 3 times a week at 7am” or “read 20 pages every day” and Cadence builds the schedule, the goal, the icon and the reminder. You always review the result before it is saved.

Connect a habit to the Health app and it completes itself: steps, active energy, exercise minutes, distance, water, mindful minutes, time asleep, the time you fell asleep, or workouts. A simple tick works too — Gym set to Workouts ticks off the moment one workout lands, and Lights out by 22:30 ticks off if you were actually asleep in time.

Read-only, opt-in per habit, and Cadence only ever asks for the metrics you have chosen. It never writes to Health.

05

No limits, no account, no cloud

Track two habits or two hundred. Everything is stored in a single readable file on your iPhone. There is no sign-up, no server, no sync service, no analytics and no advertising.

The app contains no networking code at all. There is nothing to switch off, because there is nowhere for your data to go. Export a backup whenever you like — it is plain JSON and it is yours.

The trade-off, stated plainly. Because nobody else holds a copy of your data, nobody else can recover it for you. Export a backup from Settings and keep it somewhere you trust.

07

Questions

Is there a subscription?

No. There is no subscription, no advertising and no in-app purchase. The app has no server costs to fund, because it has no server.

Does it sync between my iPhone and iPad?

No. Syncing would mean a server or an iCloud container, and the promise that nothing leaves your device is worth more than the convenience. You can move your data by exporting a backup and importing it on the other device.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Your habit data is in your device backup if you back your iPhone up to iCloud or a Mac. Beyond that, export a Cadence backup from Settings → Backup and restore. Nobody else holds a copy for you — that is the direct cost of the privacy guarantee, and it would be dishonest to bury it.

Do I need Apple Intelligence?

No. On an iPhone that has it, Apple's on-device model phrases your weekly review. On every other iPhone, Cadence's own analysis engine writes the same review from the same numbers. Nothing disappears; it just reads a little less conversationally.

Which devices does it run on?

iOS 18 or later, on iPhone and iPad. Apple Intelligence features need a device that supports it and iOS 26.
