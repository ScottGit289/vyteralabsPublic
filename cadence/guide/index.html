1. First run

Four screens, no account, nothing to sign up for.

The third screen offers a dozen starter habits and lets you type your own. Pick two or three. You can change or delete anything later, and there is no limit on how many habits you can eventually have.

The fourth screen offers reminders. They are optional, and they are local notifications only — the app never registers with Apple's push service.

The one piece of advice worth taking: pick habits you could do on your worst day. Not your best day, not a normal day — the day you slept badly, the meeting overran, and someone was ill. That is the level at which a habit survives.

2. The Today screen

The day strip across the top shows a week. Tap any day to move to it, or use the chevrons. The Today button in the top left brings you straight back.

Beneath it, a summary card shows how much of the day is logged and your overall momentum.

Then your habits.

Every interaction

What you doWhat happens
Tap the ringTicks a simple habit. Adds one to a small count. Opens the amount panel for a large one, or the timer for a timed habit. Tap again to undo.
Tap the nameOpens that habit's history and statistics.
Press and holdMenu: mark done (jumps straight to the full target), history, edit, record a slip, pause, archive.
Tap the adjust buttonFor numeric habits: larger steps, a Hit target button and a note field.
Tap the timer buttonFor timed habits: the timer, plus Log the full duration for a session you have already done.
Tap a day in the stripMoves to that day, so you can back-fill.

Mark done always means done. On a 20-page habit it logs 20 pages; on a 90-minute one it logs 90 minutes. It never undoes — use the ring, or the menu's Undo, for that.

Nothing important is hidden behind a gesture. Every action in the press-and-hold menu is also reachable from a visible control or the habit's own screen. Gestures are shortcuts, never the only route.

Perfect days

Log everything scheduled for today and the screen celebrates it — switch that off under Settings → Today screen → Celebrate perfect days if you would rather it did not.

Once you have had three such days, Cadence may ask iOS to show Apple's standard "rate this app" sheet. That sheet is Apple's, not the app's: Cadence sends no data with the request, is never told what you did with it, and will not ask again on the same version or within 120 days. Apple caps these prompts at about three a year for every app regardless. See docs/PRIVACY.md for the detail.

Ordering

Settings → Today screen → Order habits by offers four options. Needs attention first puts unfinished, slipping habits at the top — usually what you want at nine in the evening.

Groups

Give habits a group name — Training, Work, Morning — and Today sorts them under headings. Ungrouped habits stay at the top.

On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, Apple's on-device model reads the sentence. On any other iPhone, Cadence's built-in parser does. Nothing is sent anywhere either way, and you always land on the full editor to review the result before it's saved.

4. Schedules

KindMeaningBest for
Every dayDue dailyMedication, flossing, a bedtime
Certain daysDue only on the weekdays you pickGym days, bin day, a weekly review
Times per weekHit the number on any days you likeAnything that has to fit around life
Times per monthThe same over a monthLow-frequency habits
Every N daysA fixed rhythm regardless of weekdayRecovery cycles, rotations

Choose flexible when the day doesn't matter

If the real goal is "three runs a week", do not schedule Monday, Wednesday and Friday. A fixed-day schedule turns a moved run into a missed run, and your tracker starts lying to you about how you're doing.

Times-per-week habits show a running count for the current week (2/3 this week) on the right of the row, and a streak measured in weeks, not days. Three sessions on any three days keeps the streak alive.

It counts days, not sessions. "3× a week" means three separate days. Two gym sessions on the same Saturday count once, because streaks, grace days and the whole history model are built on days. If what you actually want to record is volume — sets, reps, kilometres — use a count goal, which stores a number per day, and let the schedule handle the frequency.

5. Goals: ticks, counts and timers

Simple tick

One tap. Tap again to undo. Use it for anything binary.

Reach a number

Set a target and a unit — 20 pages, 8 glasses, 8000 steps.

  • Target of 10 or less: tapping the ring adds one. Eight taps finishes eight glasses, which is the right shape for that habit.
  • Target above 10: tapping the ring opens the amount panel instead. One tap cannot plausibly finish 8,000 steps, and tapping twenty times for twenty pages is not a feature.

The panel has larger steps, a clear button, a Hit target button and a note field. Partial progress is kept and shown as a part-filled ring.

Spend time

Set a duration and use the built-in timer. It keeps counting while the app is in the background or the phone is locked, so a phone call doesn't cost you ten minutes of meditation.

You never have to sit and watch it. Under the timer, Already done it? has quick jumps sized to the goal and a Log the full duration button, so a 45-minute ride or a 90-minute work block is one tap after the fact. You can also type the exact minutes into any past day from the history screen.

6. Habits you want to break

Set the type to Break a habit and the meaning inverts: you log the days you stayed clean.

If you slip, press and hold the habit and choose Record a slip. The day is recorded honestly, with an optional note. This is deliberately not hidden — a record you can trust is more useful than a flattering streak, and the note is what tells you why it happened.

7. Streaks, momentum and grace days

The most important section in this guide.

Streak

Consecutive scheduled days completed, counting back from today. An unfinished today never breaks a streak — it shows as still open until the day ends.

Flexible schedules streak in periods: a three-times-a-week habit streaks in weeks, a four-times-a-month habit in months.

Momentum

A score from 0 to 100 that weights recent days far more than old ones. The weight halves every fortnight, so the last two weeks dominate but three strong months still cushion a bad one.

ScoreLabelWhat it means
0–24ColdThe habit has effectively stopped
25–49Warming upReal but inconsistent
50–69SteadyHappens more often than not
70–87StrongThis is a habit, not an intention
88–100Locked inProtect it. Don't add load

One missed day costs a few points, not everything, and it climbs back as soon as you resume. Over months, this is the number worth watching.

Grace days

Each habit can forgive up to five missed days per rolling thirty. The default is one. Set it to zero if you want a strict streak.

A grace day never lies to you. The missed day still shows as missed in your history, drawn with a dashed ring, and it still counts as a zero against momentum. Grace protects your motivation. Momentum reports reality. Neither is allowed to do the other's job.

Grace is spent as it's met, oldest miss first, so a day's status never changes retrospectively. What you saw last month is what you'll see next year.

8. Pausing

Holiday, injury, illness. Press and hold a habit and choose Pause for three days, a week or a fortnight.

Paused days are neither wins nor losses: they don't break the streak, they don't count against momentum, and they appear as grey squares in your history so the gap is explained rather than mysterious.

Resume now ends a pause early.

9. Correcting the record

Two routes:

  • The day strip on Today moves back a week at a time. Tap a day and log it normally.
  • Any square in a habit's history heatmap opens that day for editing — change the amount, add a note, or clear it entirely.

Correcting the record is not cheating. A tracker you can't correct becomes a tracker you stop trusting, and an untrusted tracker gets deleted.

10. Progress and statistics

The Progress tab ranks every habit by momentum.

Each habit's own screen shows:

  • Current streak, best streak, 30-day hit rate, total completions
  • Momentum bar with the fortnight's trend
  • History heatmap — four months, with a legend explaining every state
  • By day of the week — hit rate for each weekday

That last chart is the most actionable thing in the app. A habit that lands on Tuesday and falls over on Thursday has a scheduling problem, not a willpower problem, and the fix is to move it rather than to try harder.

11. The Coach

The Coach tab writes a weekly review of your last fortnight: what's working, what's slipping, the single habit worth concentrating on, and one concrete experiment for the next seven days.

It names habits and days. You will get things like "Thursday is your weakest day and Tuesday your strongest — move one habit off Thursday rather than trying to be more disciplined on it", or "When 'Walk' gets done, 'Read' also gets done 84% of the time — stack the harder one directly after the easier one."

Where the words come from

Every number the Coach states is computed by Cadence in Swift, from your own logged days. On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, Apple's on-device model is handed those facts and asked to phrase them; it is never asked to work anything out. On any other iPhone, Cadence writes the review itself from the same analysis.

The card at the bottom always tells you which engine produced it.

Your first week is different

Below about a week of history there is no trend to find, so the Coach does not pretend there is one. It will not quote you a momentum score, a hit rate or a weekday pattern built out of three days — those numbers exist arithmetically and mean nothing.

What you get instead is a read on the setup: whether the number of habits on your list is realistic, which ones have no reminder and should, which could be completing themselves from Health, and the single habit worth protecting first. In the first week this refreshes daily, because the setup is the thing that keeps changing. The full review starts once there is a week behind you.

What it is trying to do

The Coach is not there to restate numbers you can already see. Every observation it makes has to join two facts together, or say what a fact means for your plan — "four of your five misses this month were Thursdays, so that's a diary problem, not a discipline problem" rather than "Thursday is your weakest day".

It leads with what is working, named specifically, before what is slipping. Not to be nice: a habit that holds while others drift is the most useful thing in the data, because it is the one to build the rest around.

Tone

Settings → Intelligence → Tone changes how the review is written. It does not change what it finds.

ToneHow it reads
EncouragingWarm. Names what is working first and gives the win a whole sentence. A missed day is framed as a problem with the plan.
BalancedDirect and warm. Leads with what is holding up, then what is slipping. The default.
DirectLeads with the problem, with the number that shows it. Praise is cut to a clause and only where the data plainly supports it.
AnalyticalThe figure and what it implies. No encouragement and no commiseration, and a preference for comparisons over bare values.

Every tone is handed the identical facts. None of them may soften a poor fortnight into a success or harden a good one into a failure — encouragement that is not true is worse than bluntness, because you would check the numbers and stop believing the rest. What moves is which fact comes first, how much is said around it, and how a miss is framed.

The setting works on every iPhone. On one with Apple Intelligence it changes what the model is instructed to be; on any other it changes the wording Cadence uses itself. Changing it rewrites the current review straight away rather than waiting for the next refresh.

Frequency

Settings → Intelligence → Weekly review — off, weekly or daily. The regenerate button in the top right forces a fresh one any time.

It will not flatter you

If there isn't enough data, it says so rather than inventing encouragement.

12. Completing habits from Health

Some habits shouldn't need a tap. If your watch already knows you walked 9,000 steps, Cadence should just tick the box.

Turning it on

Edit any habit you're building and pick a metric under Health:

MetricUnitNotes
Stepssteps
Active energykcal
Exercise minutesminutesApple's exercise ring
Walking and running distancekm
WaterlitresOnly if something else writes it to Health
Mindful minutesminutesAny app, including Apple's Mindfulness
Time asleephoursCredited to the day you woke up
Asleep bya timeCredited to the evening it started
WorkoutsworkoutsCounts completed workouts

Cadence asks for read access the moment you save the habit — but it explains itself first. Before Apple's permission sheet appears you get a screen naming the exact metrics it will request, confirming it only ever reads, that nothing leaves the device, and what happens if you say no.

That order is deliberate. iOS asks once. Decline and it will not ask again; you would have to switch it on yourself in Settings. An explanation that arrives after the question is no use to anyone.

Cadence only ever asks for the metrics you've actually chosen. Connect another habit later and it asks for that one too, and only then.

Habits on Today show a small pink heart when they're connected.

Simple ticks count too

A habit doesn't need a numeric goal to be automated. Two cases where this is the whole point:

  • "Gym", set to Workouts. One workout in Health ticks it off. Nothing to count, nothing to tap.
  • "Lights out by 22:30", set to Asleep by. Cadence reads what time you actually fell asleep and ticks the habit off if it was at or before your target. Falling asleep at 00:40 counts against the night before, not the morning after — the habit is something you do on Tuesday night.

Because a tick's own target is just "once", Cadence asks separately how much of the metric counts as done, and shows it under the habit's name on Today: 8,000 steps, 1 workout, 22:30.

Asleep by needs something recording sleep to Health — an Apple Watch, a ring, or a sleep-tracking app. Your phone alone cannot tell when your lights went out.

How it behaves

  • Cadence reads the last fortnight every time you open the app, so a week away still catches up.
  • Health can only ever increase a day's total. Anything you logged by hand is never reduced or overwritten.
  • Access is read-only. Cadence never writes to Health.
  • Settings → Health → Sync with Health now forces a refresh.

If nothing happens

Health answers an app it cannot read with an empty result, not an error, so a blocked metric looks identical to a quiet one. After a sync, Settings → Health says which of the two it is.

To grant access after the fact: iOS Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → Cadence, and switch on the categories you want it to read.

Privacy

Health hands the numbers to the app on your iPhone, and Cadence stores them in its own local file. There is no networking code in the app, so there is no path by which any of it could go anywhere. Decline the prompt and everything else works exactly as before.

Say:

  • "What's left in Cadence"
  • "Add to a habit in Cadence"

In the Shortcuts app, Cadence offers three actions: Log a habit, Add to a habit and What's left today. Useful automations:

  • Log Walk when you arrive home.

15. Reminders

Add as many reminders as you like to a habit, each with its own time.

Reminders are local notifications scheduled by iOS on your iPhone. Cadence never registers for push notifications, so there is no device token and nothing that could be sent anywhere.

iOS allows a limited number of pending notifications per app. Cadence rebuilds its whole schedule from scratch whenever a habit changes, which is why you never end up with a stale reminder for a habit you renamed or deleted — a common complaint about other trackers. Settings → Reminders → Rebuild all reminders does it on demand.

17. Backup, restore and moving iPhone

Everything is one JSON file on your iPhone. Nobody else holds a copy — that's the point, and it's also your responsibility.

Settings → Backup and restore → Create a backup writes a readable file you can share into Files, iCloud Drive, or an email to yourself.

Restore from a backup file offers two choices:

  • Merge — adds habits and days that aren't already here. It never overwrites a day you've already logged.
  • Replace everything — wipes what's here and uses the file.

Changing iPhone

An encrypted iPhone backup or a device-to-device transfer carries Cadence across automatically. If you'd rather not rely on that, export a backup first and import it on the new device.

18. Accessibility

  • Full VoiceOver labels on every control, including the rings, the day strip and the history grid. Each history square reads its date and its state.
  • Dynamic Type throughout; layouts reflow rather than clip.
  • Colour is never the only signal. Every state also has a shape, a symbol or a label.
  • The eleven-colour palette is chosen so habits stay distinguishable with the common colour-vision deficiencies.
  • Every gesture has a visible equivalent.
  • Haptics can be switched off.

19. Troubleshooting

My streak looks wrong. Open the habit and read the history grid. A faded square is a miss covered by a grace day; a grey square is a paused day. Both keep a streak alive on purpose. If you log after midnight, set Day rolls over at to 2am or later.

A day I completed shows as missed. Check the goal. A count habit needs the full target — six of eight glasses is not a completion. Tap the day in the heatmap to correct it.

Reminders aren't arriving. Settings → Reminders. If it says blocked, allow notifications for Cadence in iPhone Settings. Then use Rebuild all reminders.

I connected a habit to Health and nothing happens. The likely cause is that Cadence was never granted read access. It asks when you save a habit with a metric on it — if you never saw that sheet, or dismissed it, go to iOS Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → Cadence and switch on the categories, then Settings → Health → Sync with Health now in Cadence. Health answers an app it can't read with an empty result rather than an error, which is why this fails silently. Also check the metric is one something actually writes: Water, Mindful minutes and Asleep by only work if a watch, ring or another app is recording them.

The Coach isn't telling me anything useful. In your first week it won't analyse trends, because there aren't any yet — it coaches your setup instead. Scores, weekday patterns and habit pairings start once there is a week of logged days behind you.

The Coach says Apple Intelligence isn't available. It needs a compatible iPhone with Apple Intelligence switched on, and the model may still be downloading. Cadence works fully without it — you get the same analysis, written by the app instead.

A widget isn't updating. Widgets refresh when you log something and periodically after. Opening the app forces a refresh. If a widget shows nothing, check the habits are scheduled for today.

I deleted a habit by mistake. If you exported a backup, restore it and choose Merge. If not, it's gone — that is the honest cost of holding no copy of your data. Archiving, rather than deleting, keeps everything.
