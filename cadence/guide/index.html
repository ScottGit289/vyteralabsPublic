Cadence

User Guide

Publisher: Vytera Labs Ltd  ·  For: Cadence on iPhone and iPad, iOS 18 or later  ·  info@vyteralabs.com

Cadence is a habit tracker built to survive a bad week. This guide covers how it thinks, how to set it up properly, and the handful of settings that stop it behaving in ways you did not expect.

If you read nothing else
  • Cadence counts days, not sessions. Two gym sessions on one Saturday count once.
  • Streak and momentum are two different numbers doing two different jobs. Neither is allowed to do the other's.
  • Grace days protect your motivation, never your data — a forgiven day still shows as missed and still counts as a zero.
  • There is no cloud and no sync. Export a backup from Settings and keep it somewhere you trust.

1. Your first habit

Type what you want in plain English — "gym 3 times a week at 7am", or "read 20 pages every day" — and Cadence builds the schedule, the goal, the icon and the reminder for you.

You always review the result before it is saved. If it has read you wrongly, change it there rather than living with it.

There is no limit on how many habits you keep. Track two or two hundred.

2. The two numbers

Most trackers give you a streak and then punish you with it: miss one day and months of work reads as zero. That single design choice is why people quit. Cadence splits the job in two.

Streak

Consecutive days, counted honestly. It is the number that keeps you moving, and it is deliberately unforgiving in the ordinary case.

Momentum

Time-weighted consistency out of 100, with a half-life of a fortnight. One missed day costs a few points rather than everything, and it climbs back the moment you resume.

Read them together. A broken streak with momentum in the eighties means you had one bad day, not a collapse.

3. Grace days and pausing

Give a habit one or two grace days a month and a single miss will not end the run.

What grace does not do is rewrite the past. The missed day still shows as missed in your history, and it still counts as a zero against your momentum. A tracker that quietly flatters you is one you stop believing.

Pausing is the right tool for a holiday, an injury or illness. Paused days are neither wins nor losses, and they do not break a streak.

In the history grid: a faded square is a miss covered by a grace day, and a grey square is a paused day. Both keep a streak alive on purpose.

4. Schedules and goals

Five schedules

  • Every day
  • Chosen weekdays
  • Times per week
  • Times per month
  • Every N days

For a "3× a week" habit, log it on three separate days. The count on the right of the row — 2/3 this week — shows where you are.

Ticks, counts and timers

A habit can be a simple tick, a count with a numeric target, or a timed session with a background-safe timer.

A habit with a numeric target needs the full target to complete: six of eight glasses is progress, not a completion.

If you want to record volume rather than frequency, use a count goal, which stores a number per day.

Logging a timed habit after the fact

Open the timer and use Already done it? underneath. It has quick jumps and a button that logs the full duration outright, so a 45-minute ride is one tap afterwards. You can also type exact minutes into any past day from the habit's history screen.

Build or break

Cadence handles habits you want to start and habits you want to stop. For the second kind, record a slip honestly rather than losing the day.

5. Habits that tick themselves off

Connect a habit to the Health app and it completes itself. Available metrics are steps, active energy, exercise minutes, distance, water, mindful minutes, time asleep, the time you fell asleep, and workouts.

A Gym habit set to Workouts ticks off the moment a workout lands. Lights out by 22:30 ticks off only if you were actually asleep in time.

Access is read-only and opt-in per habit. Cadence only ever asks for the metrics you have chosen, and it never writes to Health.

If a Health habit never completes

The usual cause is that read access was never granted. Cadence asks when you save a habit with a metric on it — if you never saw that sheet, or dismissed it, go to iOS Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → Cadence, switch on the categories, then use Settings → Health → Sync with Health now in Cadence. Health answers an app it cannot read with an empty result rather than an error, which is why this fails silently.

Also check that something is actually writing the metric. Water, mindful minutes and "asleep by" only work if a watch, a ring or another app is recording them. Nothing can tell when your lights went out unless something was measuring.

6. The weekly review

Cadence reads your last fortnight and writes a short, specific review: what is working, what is slipping, the one habit worth concentrating on, and a concrete experiment to run for seven days.

It names habits and it names days. It will tell you that Thursday is where your training falls over, or that your reading always happens on days you walk.

On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, Apple's on-device model writes it. On every other iPhone, Cadence's own analysis engine does, so the feature never simply disappears. Either way every number it quotes is computed by the app from your own logged days — the model is asked to phrase facts, never to work them out.

In your first week it will not pretend to find trends that do not exist yet. It coaches your setup instead, and starts scoring once there is a week behind you.

7. Widgets, Siri and reminders

Interactive widgets: tap a ring on the Home Screen to log without opening the app. Widgets refresh when you log something and periodically after; opening the app forces a refresh. If a widget shows nothing, check the habits are actually scheduled for today.

Siri and Shortcuts: say "Log Read", or compose Cadence into your own automations.

Reminders are local notifications scheduled by iOS on your device. Nothing is registered with Apple's push servers. If they stop arriving, open Settings → Reminders in Cadence; if it says blocked, allow notifications in iOS Settings and then use Rebuild all reminders. Because they are local, a Focus mode or Scheduled Summary can also hold them back.

8. History and corrections

You get a history heatmap, a weekday breakdown and per-habit detail. Any day can be back-filled or corrected — tap the day in the heatmap.

If a day you completed shows as missed, check the goal first: a numeric target needs the full target.

Archive rather than delete. Archiving keeps the whole history and just hides the habit. Deleting is final — if you have not exported a backup, it is gone.

9. Settings worth knowing about

Day rolls over at

If you tend to log after midnight, set Settings → Day rolls over at to 2am or later, so a late entry counts against the day you meant. This is the single most common cause of a streak looking wrong.

Accessibility

Full VoiceOver support, Dynamic Type, and no state signalled by colour alone.

The built-in guide

The complete user guide also ships inside the app, so it works with no signal.

10. Backup, restore and erase

Everything lives in a single readable file on your device. There is no sign-up, no server, no sync service, no analytics and no advertising — the app contains no networking code at all.

That has a direct cost, and it is worth stating plainly: because nobody else holds a copy of your data, nobody else can recover it for you.

  • Export: Settings → Backup and restore → Create a backup. Plain JSON, readable in any text editor.
  • Restore or merge: Settings → Backup and restore → Restore. Merge keeps what is already there, which is also how you recover a habit you deleted by mistake.
  • Erase: Settings → Erase all data, or delete the app.

Moving to a new device works the same way: export on the old one, import on the new one. There is no sync, by design.

11. When something is wrong

Cadence collects no diagnostics and sends us nothing, so a bug report is only as good as what you tell us. Include your device model and iOS version (Settings → General → About), the Cadence version (Settings → About), what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. A screenshot helps if it is something you can see.

Please do not send your backup file unless we ask — it contains your entire habit history.

info@vyteralabs.com — replies within two working days, UK hours. The support page covers the common problems in more detail, and the privacy policy explains exactly why there is nothing for us to hold.
