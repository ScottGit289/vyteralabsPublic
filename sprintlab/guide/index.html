SprintLab

User Guide

Publisher: Vytera Labs Ltd  ·  For: SprintLab on iPhone  ·  info@vyteralabs.com

SprintLab keeps your times honestly, tells you each morning what kind of session your body is ready for, and finds the patterns in your own data that explain your best days. Everything runs on your phone — there is no account and nothing to sign in to.

If you read nothing else
  • The whole app works on manual entry. A wearable only auto-fills numbers you could type yourself.
  • Every metric is labelled with where it came from, so you always know what a score is built on.
  • Timing methods and start types are kept in separate record books — a hand time can never overwrite an electronic one.
  • Your data lives only on this device. Export a backup periodically; nobody else holds a copy.

2. Setting up your athlete profile

There is no sign-up step — open the app and it is ready. Start with Settings → Edit athlete profile so age grading and the readiness baselines have something to work from.

Then log one thing you already know: a recent personal best, or yesterday's session. Every analysis in the app is built from your own logged data rather than assumptions about an average athlete, so it gets sharper the more you give it.

3. Performance Readiness

Each day the Ready tab produces a 0–100 readiness score and one plain training call.

What the call means

ScoreCallWhat it means
80–100SprintPrimed for quality speed work or a time trial.
60–79GymGood for strength and power work; hold back top-end speed volume.
40–59RecoveryKeep it light — tempo, technique or mobility only.
Below 40RestPrioritise rest or very light movement today.

Two rules are worth knowing. If you have logged an injury or niggle in the last few days, the app will never tell you to sprint. And if it has no recovery data at all to read, it defaults to Gym rather than guessing at speed work.

What goes into the score

It fuses whatever it has, and the weights renormalise over the inputs that are actually present — so a fully manual day still gets a real score:

  • Your wearable's own recovery or readiness score, and its sleep score.
  • Sleep you rated yourself when logging a session, from 1 (poor) to 5 (great).
  • HRV and resting heart rate, each compared against your personal baseline rather than a population average.
  • Body temperature deviation and stress balance.
  • Your training load — the last 7 days against your 28-day weekly average, so a recent spike shows up.
  • Your own check-in on leg freshness, energy and motivation, each 1–10.
  • Whether you have reported a recent injury.

The dashboard explains itself rather than just showing a number: depending on the day it headlines Why you're ready, Why today's a measured go, or Why to hold back today, with the drivers listed most significant first.

Tap any metric to enter or edit it. The score updates immediately.

4. Where each number comes from

Every metric carries its source, so you can judge how much to trust a score:

SourceMeaning
ManualYou typed it. A manual value always wins.
Oura, Garmin, AppleSynced from that source.
CalculatedDerived from other values you have supplied.
EstimatedInferred. The weakest of the four — treat a score built mostly on estimates with proportionate caution.

When you edit a synced metric by hand, the original reading is kept underneath, so you can put it back.

5. Connecting a wearable

Entirely optional. SprintLab supports Apple Health, Oura and Garmin. Go to Settings → Manage wearables.

Apple Health is read natively on the device through HealthKit. Oura and Garmin use the vendor's own secure sign-in, so SprintLab never sees your password, and your health data comes straight to your phone.

Once connected you can Sync now at any time, or leave it on an automatic schedule. Disconnect whenever you like. For Apple Health you can also review or revoke access in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health.

6. Personal bests

Quick-pick distances run 30m, 50m, 60m, 80m, 100m, 150m, 200m, 300m and 400m, and you can add custom distances for sessions that do not fit a track.

Records are kept in separate books

A record is identified by distance and start type and timing method together. That is deliberate: comparing a flying 30 with a block 30, or a stopwatch time with electronic timing, tells you nothing.

FlyingPhone (HT)
Estimate (est)

Wind

Record the wind with the time; a tailwind is positive. For sprints up to and including 200m, a tailwind over +2.0 m/s makes the time wind-aided: it is kept and flagged, but never becomes a legal personal best. Wind is irrelevant to 300m and 400m.

One nuance worth knowing: if you leave the wind blank it counts as legal. The app will not invalidate a time on a reading you never took, so record the wind when you have it.

You can hide wind-aided results entirely with Settings → Show wind-aided results.

When you set a personal best, the app tells you exactly which time you beat.

7. The training log

Log a session from the Train tab: session type (Sprint, Gym, Tempo, Recovery or Other), timed reps, gym work, notes, injuries and sleep quality.

Out of that you get progress graphs per distance, PB history with improvement deltas, an improving/stable/declining trend, weekly session volume, and a 0–100 consistency score that rewards showing up week after week rather than one heroic session a month.

Logging sleep quality and any niggle matters more than it looks: both feed tomorrow's readiness score.

8. Analytics

SprintLab looks for the relationships that matter without being asked — sleep against 100m time, HRV against top speed, stress against performance, gym load against speed — and reports correlations, trends, and the recovery conditions your personal bests actually happen in.

That last one is the practically useful output, because it tells you what to reproduce before a race.

The analysis grows with your data rather than assuming it on day one. Early on it will have little to say; that is honest rather than broken.

9. Sprint calculator and age grading

Sprint calculator

Average speed in m/s, km/h and mph; equal-speed equivalents across 30, 60, 100, 150, 200, 300 and 400m; and even-pace split targets.

Age grading

WMA-style age-graded scoring for 60m, 100m, 200m and 400m, computed entirely offline from approximate tables. Useful for masters athletes, when the raw clock stops being the interesting number. Other distances are not age-graded.

10. On-device intelligence

Settings → Use on-device intelligence lets Apple Intelligence write your insights and daily briefing. It runs entirely on your iPhone: nothing is sent to Apple, to us, or to anyone else, and it is never used for advertising or model training.

It needs a compatible iPhone with Apple Intelligence enabled. On any other device — or if the model is still downloading — SprintLab uses its built-in coach instead and works exactly the same. The app tells you which one produced what you are reading; the fallback is never silent.

12. Backup, restore and delete

Your data is stored on your device, so backing it up is your job rather than ours. That is the direct cost of there being no server, and it is worth stating plainly.

  • Export backup hands a copy to the iOS share sheet — Files, AirDrop or email.
  • Import backup asks Restore from this backup? and offers Replace my data or Keep my data. Read that prompt carefully: Replace discards what is currently on the device.
  • Delete all data confirms twice — it is permanent. Deleting the app removes everything too.

Export a backup periodically. There is no cloud copy and nobody else holds one for you.

13. When something is wrong

SprintLab collects no diagnostics and sends us nothing, so a report is only as good as what you tell us. Include your device model and iOS version (iOS Settings → General → About), the SprintLab version from its own Settings screen, what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. A screenshot helps if it is something you can see.

Please do not send your backup export unless we ask — it contains your whole training history. If a problem genuinely needs it we will say so, and explain what we will do with it and when we will delete it.
