SprintLab

User Guide

Publisher: Vytera Labs Ltd  ·  For: SprintLab on iPhone  ·  info@vyteralabs.com

SprintLab does three things: it keeps your times honestly, it tells you each morning what kind of session your body is ready for, and it finds the patterns in your own data that explain your best days. Everything runs on your phone. There is no account to create and nothing to sign in to.

If you read nothing else
  • You can use the whole app with manual entry. A wearable is optional and only auto-fills numbers you could type yourself.
  • Every metric is labelled by where it came from — Manual, Oura, Calculated or Estimated — so you always know what a score is built on.
  • Hand times and electronic times never mix, and a tailwind over +2.0 m/s never becomes a legal personal best.
  • Your data lives only on your device. Export a backup periodically — nobody else holds a copy for you.

1. Getting started

There is no sign-up step. Open the app and it is ready. Because there is no account, there is also no password to forget and no server holding your training history.

Start by logging one thing you already know — a recent personal best, or yesterday's session. The app is more useful the more you give it, and every analysis in it is built from your own logged data rather than assumptions about an average athlete.

2. Personal bests, kept honestly

SprintLab covers every standard distance from 30m to 400m, plus custom distances for sessions that do not fit a track.

Four rules keep the records meaningful, and they are enforced rather than suggested:

Timing method

Electronic and hand times are kept separate and never mixed. A hand time cannot overwrite an electronic record, which stops a generous stopwatch quietly replacing a real mark.

Wind

Record the wind reading with the time. For sprints up to 200m, a tailwind over +2.0 m/s is automatically flagged as wind-aided. The time is kept and shown, but it never counts as a legal personal best. If you would rather not see them at all, wind-aided results can be hidden in Settings.

Start type

Block, standing and flying starts are tracked separately, because comparing a flying 30 with a block 30 tells you nothing.

What you beat

When you set a personal best, the celebration tells you exactly which time you beat — not simply that something improved.

3. The daily readiness score

Each day SprintLab produces a 0–100 readiness score and a single call to go with it: Sprint, Gym, Recovery or Rest.

The score blends your recovery signals — sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, body temperature and stress — with your own check-in on energy, motivation and leg freshness, and your recent training load.

Editing a metric

Tap any metric on the Readiness screen to enter or edit it. The score updates instantly. A manual edit always wins, but the original wearable reading is kept underneath — open the metric again and tap Use Oura value to restore it.

Knowing what the number is built on

Every value carries a provenance label: Manual, Oura, Calculated or Estimated. A readiness score built mostly on estimates deserves less weight than one built on measured data, and the app does not hide which you have.

The score is also expected to explain itself — "today matches your previous personal-best days", or "HRV is 12% above your monthly average". A number without a reason is not much use on a Tuesday morning.

4. Connecting a wearable

This is entirely optional. The whole app works on manual entry alone, and you can disconnect at any time.

Oura

Go to Settings → Wearables, turn on Enable wearable sync, then tap Connect Oura Ring and sign in on Oura's own official page. SprintLab never sees your Oura password, and your health data comes straight to your phone. You can set how often it auto-syncs.

Sign-in is relayed through a small SprintLab-operated service so that Oura's confidential app secret never has to ship inside the app. It brokers the sign-in exchange only, keeps nothing, and never receives your health data. The privacy policy covers it in full.

Apple Health

Go to Settings → Wearables, enable sync, tap Connect Apple Health, and tap Allow on Apple's permission sheet. SprintLab reads sleep, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate and blood oxygen entirely on your device. Nothing leaves your phone.

You can review or revoke that access at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health.

5. The training log

A session takes seconds to log: type, timed reps, gym work, notes, injuries and sleep.

From that you get progress graphs for each distance, trends that show where you are improving and where you are sliding, and a consistency score that rewards showing up week after week rather than one heroic session a month.

6. Analytics

SprintLab looks for the relationships that matter without being asked: sleep against 100m time, HRV against top speed, stress against performance, gym load against speed.

It reports correlations, trends, and the recovery conditions your personal bests actually happen in — which is the practical question, because it tells you what to reproduce before a race.

The analysis grows with your data rather than assuming it on day one. Early on it will have little to say. That is the honest answer, not a fault.

7. The calculator and age grading

Sprint calculator

Speeds in m/s, km/h and mph, equal-speed equivalents across distances, and even-pace splits.

Age grading

WMA-style age grading for masters athletes, computed entirely offline. Useful when the raw clock stops being the interesting number.

8. On-device intelligence

An optional setting — Settings → Intelligence, off by default — that uses Apple Intelligence to write your insights and daily briefing.

It runs entirely on your iPhone. Nothing is sent to Apple, to us, or to anyone else, and it is never used for advertising or model training.

It needs a compatible iPhone with Apple Intelligence enabled. On other devices SprintLab uses its built-in engine instead and works exactly the same. You can turn it off at any time.

9. Backup, restore and delete

Your data is stored on your device, so backing up is your job rather than ours. This is the direct cost of there being no server, and it is worth stating plainly.

  • Export: Settings → Export backup hands a copy to the iOS share sheet — Files, AirDrop or email.
  • Restore: on the other device, use Import backup and choose that file.
  • Delete: Settings → Delete all data, or simply delete the app. Everything is on your device, so there is nothing held elsewhere to remove.

Export a backup periodically. There is no cloud copy, and nobody else holds one for you.

10. When something is wrong

SprintLab collects no diagnostics and sends us nothing, so a report is only as good as what you tell us. Include your device model and iOS version (Settings → General → About), the SprintLab version from the app's own Settings screen, what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. A screenshot helps if it is something you can see.

Please do not send your backup export unless we ask — it contains your whole training history. If a problem genuinely needs it we will say so, and explain what we will do with it and when we will delete it.
