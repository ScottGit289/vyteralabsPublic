Sinew

User Guide

Publisher: Vytera Labs Ltd  ·  For: Sinew on iPhone  ·  info@vyteralabs.com

Sinew is a strength and cardio workout tracker that stays on your phone. Pick an exercise by muscle group, log sets in two taps, and let the app tell you — honestly — whether you are progressing. Everything runs on your device: there is no account and nothing to sign in to.

If you read nothing else
  • Sinew is free for 7 days from your first logged session, then Sinew Pro keeps it going. Nothing is held back in those 7 days, and nothing you log ever leaves your phone.
  • Every verdict — progressing, plateau, regressing — is measured against that exercise's own history, with the number shown beside it.
  • Your weight increment and units are yours to set. Do it first, in Settings, and every stepper in the app follows.
  • Your data lives only on this device. Export a backup periodically; nobody else holds a copy.

1. Getting started

There is no sign-up step — open the app and it is ready. The app opens on the Train tab.

WhereWhat it is for
HomeToday at a glance: your streak, this week's sets and cardio minutes, quick-start buttons and your last workout. With Oura connected (Pro) it also carries today's readiness call.
TrainStart a workout, log it, and look back through your history.
CardioLog runs, rides, rows, swims, walks and intervals; weekly load, pace trends and best efforts.
ProgressExercise trends, muscle balance, suggestions and the readout.
SettingsUnits and increment, rest timer, theme, Apple Health, Wearables, Intelligence, Sinew Pro, backup and deletion, support.

Two things are worth doing before your first session. In Settings, choose your weight unit (kg or lb) and the weight increment the steppers should move by — 0.5, 1, 1.25, 2.5 or 5 — so that logging matches the plates in your gym. Then set the rest timer to the seconds you actually rest for, or switch it off.

After that, log one workout. Every analysis in the app is built from your own sessions rather than from assumptions about an average lifter, so it gets sharper the more you give it — and it will say little until it has something to compare.

2. Logging a workout, step by step

  1. Start. On the Train tab, tap Start workout. Begin blank, or pick one of your templates.
  2. Pick an exercise by muscle group. Tap Add exercise. The full-screen picker is grouped by muscle-group chips — Chest, Back, Shoulders, Biceps, Triceps, Forearms, Core, Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes, Calves and Full body — with search, your recent exercises, and the option to create a custom exercise if the one you need is not in the library of more than 150.
  3. Use last. The exercise appears with last session's sets greyed in. Tap Use last to fill them in as a starting point, then change whatever is different today.
  4. Add sets. Each set row has a weight stepper that moves by your chosen increment, a reps stepper, and a tick to mark the set done. RPE is optional.
  5. Rest. After you tick a set, the rest timer starts on its own and counts down the seconds you set. If you have turned it off in Settings, nothing happens and you carry on.
  6. Finish. Tap Finish. The summary shows your volume, any personal bests hit and the change in estimated 1RM for each exercise, and the workout is saved.
  7. Apple Health. If you have connected Apple Health and turned on Write workouts, the completed workout is written to Health as well. It is opt-in and off until you turn it on; see section 5.

The workout in progress is saved after every change. If the phone dies, a call comes in or iOS closes the app, you pick up exactly where you were.

3. Templates and history

A template is a saved list of exercises — “Push day”, say — that you can start a workout from, so a routine you repeat does not have to be rebuilt each time.

The quickest way to make one is from a workout you have already done: open it in the History list on the Train tab and choose Duplicate as template. From then on it appears as a choice when you tap Start workout.

History lists every saved workout with per-workout detail. Swipe a workout to delete it.

4. Cardio

On the Cardio tab, tap to log a session: the activity (run, bike, row, swim, walk, HIIT or other), duration, distance, average heart rate, RPE and notes. Distance follows the unit you set in Settings, kilometres or miles.

Out of that you get your weekly load in minutes and distance, a pace trend for each activity, and your best efforts. The trend reads all of your history.

Importing from Apple Health

Workouts recorded by an Apple Watch or by another app can be imported from Apple Health, so a tracked run does not have to be typed in again. Imports are de-duplicated: a workout that is already in Sinew is not added a second time. Connect Apple Health first, as described next.

5. Apple Health

Entirely optional, and read natively on the device through HealthKit — no sign-in, no relay, no network. Go to Settings → Apple Health, tap Connect, and tap Allow for the categories you want to share on Apple's permission sheet.

Sinew asks to read workouts, body weight, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability and sleep, and uses them only for its own features on your device. It asks to write only one thing: your completed workouts, and only once you turn on Write workouts in the same screen.

You can review or revoke any of this at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health, or in the Health app under Sharing → Apps → Sinew. Permissions are granted per data type, so if an import seems to be missing something, check that the particular type is switched on there.

6. Reading your trends

The Progress tab opens on Exercise trends: every exercise you have logged, each with a trend arrow and the gap between your last session and its trend. Tap one for the detail screen — a chart of estimated 1RM over time, your top set, your volume, and the assessment in plain words.

What “e1RM” is

Your estimated one-rep max is the heaviest single lift a set implies, so that a set of 5 and a set of 8 at different weights can be put on the same scale. Sinew uses the Epley formula:

e1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)

So 100 kg for 5 reps gives an e1RM of 100 × (1 + 5 ÷ 30) ≈ 116.7 kg, and 90 kg for 10 reps gives 120 kg — the second set implies the stronger lifter, even though the bar was lighter. It is an estimate, best for moderate rep ranges, and it is the same formula for everyone, which is what makes your sessions comparable with each other.

What “vs trend” means

Sinew fits a trend line through an exercise's e1RM over all of your history. Your latest session is then compared with where that line says you should be, and the difference is shown as a percentage. 2.1% above trend means last session was slightly ahead of your own recent trajectory; a figure below trend means it fell short of it. Top set and volume are tracked alongside, so a day where the bar was lighter but the work was greater is not misread.

The three assessments

AssessmentWhat it meansWhat the app suggests
ProgressingThe trend is rising and your recent sessions are keeping up with it.Keep going. Whatever you are doing is working; change nothing yet.
PlateauThe trend has flattened. Sessions are landing on it, but it is not moving.Add a rep to your working sets, or change the rep range, to give the exercise a new stimulus.
RegressingThe trend is falling, or recent sessions are consistently below it.Check your recovery — sleep, stress, how much else you are training — and consider a deload week before pushing on.

The assessment always carries the number behind it, in the form “Bench press is progressing: e1RM +4.2% over 6 weeks; last session 2.1% above trend.” Read the figure, not just the word: a plateau at a weight you have only just reached is a very different thing from a plateau that has lasted two months.

Sinew Pro adds a progression forecast to each exercise, projecting the trend forward.

7. Muscle balance

Progress → Muscle balance counts your hard sets this week for each muscle group and sets them against a target band of 10 to 20 sets a week, an evidence-based range for building strength and muscle. Groups below the band are flagged as under-trained.

The sets are counted from the exercises you log, by the muscle group they belong to, so a muscle you train with several exercises is credited with all of them. A 12-week view is where a chronic gap — the shoulders that have been under the band since spring — becomes visible.

The band is a target, not an order. If your programme deliberately keeps a group low for a block, a flag there is expected.

8. Suggestions by area

Progress → Suggest asks which area you want to improve, then ranks exercises for it. The order favours the movement patterns you have trained least recently and keeps the equipment varied, and every suggestion carries a reason, so you can see why it is there and disagree with it if you like.

With Sinew Pro and an Oura Ring connected, the ranking is also adjusted by today's readiness: on a day the ring says to go lighter, the plan is tuned accordingly. See section 10.

9. The readout and on-device intelligence

Progress → Readout is a narrative of where your training stands, written by the app's built-in coach — a rules-based engine that assesses every trend. It works on any iPhone the app runs on.

With Sinew Pro, Settings → Intelligence lets Apple Intelligence write that narrative instead. It runs entirely on your iPhone using Apple's Foundation Models: nothing is sent to Apple, to us, or to anyone else, the app makes no network request to do it, and it is never used for advertising or model training.

It is off by default. It needs a compatible iPhone with Apple Intelligence enabled. On any other device — or if the model is not available — Sinew uses its built-in coach instead and works exactly the same. The readout is clearly labelled with which one produced it; the fallback is never silent.

10. Oura Ring (Pro)

Entirely optional. With a ring connected, your readiness, sleep and HRV are turned into one daily training call that appears on the Home tab before you train.

Connecting

  1. Go to Settings → Wearables.
  2. Turn on Enable wearable sync.
  3. Tap Connect Oura.
  4. Sign in on Oura's own page and approve the data you want to share. Sinew never sees your Oura password.

Your health data is fetched directly from Oura to your phone and stored there. The only piece of Vytera Labs infrastructure involved is a small sign-in relay that completes the sign-in exchange, keeps nothing, and never receives your health data; the privacy policy describes it in full. You can disconnect at any time from the same screen.

The daily call

CallWhat it means
Go heavyRecovery looks good. A day for your heaviest or hardest work.
NormalTrain as planned.
LighterRecovery is below par. Keep the session, but take the load or the volume down.
RestPrioritise rest or very light movement today.

The call is informational. It is built from the ring's readings and does not know how you feel; if the two disagree, trust yourself.

12. Backup, import and delete

Your data is stored on your device, so backing it up is your job rather than ours. That is the direct cost of there being no server, and it is worth stating plainly.

  • Export backup hands a copy to the iOS share sheet — Files, AirDrop or email.
  • Import backup reads that file back in, on this device or a new one. Read the prompt carefully before confirming.
  • Delete all data is permanent. Deleting the app removes everything too.

Export a backup periodically. There is no cloud copy and nobody else holds one for you. A backup never contains your Oura sign-in: that is held in the device keychain and stays there.

13. Sinew Pro

Sinew is free for 7 days from your first logged session, with every feature open, no purchase and no card. The clock starts when you save your first workout or cardio session; Home and Settings show how many days are left. When the 7 days end, the app shows the Sinew Pro screen and nothing else until you choose a plan or restore an earlier purchase. Everything you logged stays on your phone and can be exported from that screen.

Sinew Pro is the whole app: unlimited workouts and cardio, the full exercise library, custom exercises, templates, the rest timer, Apple Health, trends over all of your history with the built-in coach, a 12-week muscle-balance view and suggestions, the Oura Ring integration, readiness-adjusted suggestions, the Apple Intelligence readout, and backups.

Buying it

Settings → Sinew Pro shows your status and the three plans: a monthly subscription at £3.99, an annual subscription at £24.99, or a lifetime purchase at £49.99 that never renews. Both subscriptions begin with a 7-day free trial. Prices are shown in the App Store and may differ by territory. Apple takes the payment; we never see your card or your Apple ID. Family Sharing is on for all three.

Restoring and managing

  • New phone, or reinstalled the app? Tap Restore purchases on the same screen. Your entitlement is checked on the device with Apple's StoreKit; there is no Sinew account to sign in to.
  • Cancelling a subscription is done with Apple, not with us: iOS Settings → your name → Subscriptions → Sinew, or tap Manage subscription in the app, which takes you there. A subscription renews unless it is cancelled at least 24 hours before the current period ends.
  • Refunds are handled by Apple under its own terms.

14. When something is wrong

Sinew collects no diagnostics and sends us nothing on its own, so a report is only as good as what you tell us. Include your device model and iOS version (iOS Settings → General → About), the Sinew version from its own Settings screen, what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. A screenshot helps if it is something you can see.

If the app has crashed, Settings → Support can email us a crash report. You see the email before it is sent, and nothing goes anywhere unless you send it.

Please do not send your backup export unless we ask — it contains your whole training history. If a problem genuinely needs it we will say so, and explain what we will do with it and when we will delete it.
